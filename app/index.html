The turbine, its digital twin, and the value in between

A 126-metre rotor spins in turbulent wind next to its virtual copy. The twin computes, from physics, what the turbine should be doing. The gap between expected and measured behaviour exposes a failing bearing weeks early, finds energy lost to a misaligned nacelle, keeps an iced rotor producing and stretches the tower's fatigue life.

A twin is only as useful as the ecosystem around it

The physics model alone does nothing. Value appears when sensors, data, the model, analytics and people who can act are connected in a loop that returns to the physical turbine.

Inside the loop

SCADA (10-minute averages of about 40 signals), vibration monitoring at high sampling rate, strain gauges, weather forecasts and maintenance history flow into one data platform. The physics twin turns every record into an expected value; analytics watch the residuals.

Around the loop

The manufacturer's service team, crane and logistics providers, spare-parts suppliers, the grid and power market, insurers and the owner's asset managers all use the same twin. A bearing warning becomes a parts order, a crane booking and a low-wind repair window.

Across the fleet

One twin learns from one turbine. A fleet of twins compares similar machines, so a pattern seen on one unit becomes an early check on all of them. This is where a digital twin ecosystem compounds its value.

How the physics works

Every number on this page is computed in the browser from the equations below. The turbine and its data are simulated; the parameters are those of a real, published reference turbine.

Turbine and aerodynamics

NREL 5 MW reference turbine, land-based: rotor radius 63 m, hub height 90 m, rated 5 MW at 11.4 m/s and 12.1 rpm, gearbox 97:1, generator efficiency 94.4 %. Blade geometry uses the published chord and twist at 17 stations.

P = ½ ρ A U³ Cp(λ, β) · η,   λ = ωR / U

Cp(λ, β) is Heier's empirical surface scaled to the rotor's optimum (Cp 0.482 at λ 7.55); pitch is mapped so that steady-state pitch angles reproduce the NREL table (3.8° at 12 m/s, 10.5° at 15 m/s, 23.5° at 25 m/s). Thrust follows from momentum theory, giving about 780 kN at rated wind.

Control and dynamics

J dω/dt = Qaero − N·Qgen,   m ẍ + c ẋ + k x = Fthrust(U − ẋ)

Below rated, generator torque tracks the optimum (Q = Kω²); above rated, a gain-scheduled PI pitch controller with the NREL baseline gains holds 12.1 rpm, limited to 8°/s. The tower's first fore-aft mode (0.32 Hz, 1 % damping) is driven by thrust, with aerodynamic damping from the relative wind. Turbulence is an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with the IEC Kaimal length scale (340 m).

From records to decisions

Every 10 minutes the simulated turbine produces a SCADA record with sensor noise. The twin evaluates the same physics with the measured wind and air density, assuming a healthy, aligned and clean rotor. Residuals feed simple, explainable detectors:

  • Bearing: bearing-defect frequency in the vibration envelope, then an EWMA of the temperature residual; an exponential fit gives remaining life and the repair is placed in the lowest-wind window of the forecast.
  • Yaw: rolling 3-day power ratio in partial load; a deficit with a 5-sigma margin is converted into an angle via the cos² law.
  • Icing: power residual below 85 % at sub-zero temperature triggers blade heating instead of a stop.
  • Fatigue: tower-base damage-equivalent load from thrust variance under turbulence, Wöhler exponent 4, accumulated with Miner's rule and calibrated to a 20-year design life.
